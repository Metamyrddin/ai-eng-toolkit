## How to read these results (hand-written)

* **MinHash estimator:** the signature estimate of Jaccard similarity has near-zero bias, and its standard deviation is close to (a little below) the theoretical `sqrt(J(1-J)/m)` at J = 0.5 — i.e. the implementation is statistically correct.
* **Recall < 1 is expected, not a bug.** With `b` bands of `r` rows the candidate probability at similarity J is `1-(1-J^r)^b`; ours (see table, bands × rows) is tuned so J ≈ 0.85 is the 50 % point, so pairs just above the 0.8 threshold are often missed while pairs above ~0.9 are almost always found. Choose (b, r) for your recall/precision needs (the "S-curve" analysis is in *Mining of Massive Datasets* by Leskovec, Rajaraman & Ullman, ch. 3 — no link given because the book's site did not respond when we checked). Our implementation and `datasketch` reach the same recall on the same planted pairs; they differ by a handful of removals at the margin.
* **Timing comparison caution:** our from-scratch version is faster than `datasketch` in this run, but the two use different data structures and `datasketch` was driven in a simple insert-and-query loop; this is *not* a fair library benchmark.
* **Quality filters:** the heuristics catch every planted garbage type, and pass almost all real FineWeb documents (FineWeb is already filtered, so a high pass rate is expected). The **perplexity** result is the instructive one: AUC ≈ 1 only for garbage that is *less* predictable than normal text (random symbols, shuffled words); AUC ≈ 0 for repetition, all-caps spam and keyword lists, which a language model finds *easier* than real text (a low perplexity). A perplexity filter must therefore be two-sided or combined with heuristics. The planted types are synthetic: real web spam is harder.
* **Streaming pipeline:** the table reports peak RSS, and `roundtrip_ok` shows the shards read back to exactly the tokens written. The run re-injected 100 earlier documents verbatim; `dropped_duplicate` counts the documents removed by dedup. We did not trace why this is not exactly 100 (a likely, untested explanation: some injected copies were of documents already rejected by the quality filter, which are counted under `dropped_quality`).
